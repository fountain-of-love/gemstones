# Amber: Characteristics and Effects

## Overview

Amber is hardened ancient tree resin rather than a crystal or mineral. It feels warm in the hand, burns with a sharp resinous smell, and becomes electrically charged when rubbed. The material can preserve recognizable insects, seeds, plant fragments, air bubbles, and other inclusions.

Baltic amber is associated with the Eocene and is commonly described as roughly 40–50 million years old. Younger fossil resin—especially material around two million years old or less—is better called [[Copal]], although commercial naming is not always careful.

The [[#Composition|composition]] is organic and source-specific: C<sub>10</sub>H<sub>16</sub>O + (H<sub>2</sub>S). The important identity distinction is not a mineral formula but amber versus copal, treated resin, and imitations.

The [[#Appearance|appearance]] ranges from honey-yellow and transparent to brown, milky, red, green, or blue-fluorescing material. Inclusions and microscopic air bubbles strongly affect the visual character.

## Geology And Occurrence

Amber began as plant resin that hardened and matured through geological time. Baltic material is associated with Eocene deposits. It is often recovered as small pieces, including material washed onto beaches, while large museum specimens are exceptional.

## History And Cultural Use

Amber beads and cult objects occur in prehistoric contexts, including graves more than 8,000 years old. In the Stone and Bronze Ages, amber was used for jewelry, incense, talismans, and exchange. A Baltic amber trade network connected northern material with central and southern Europe. Amber is also the national stone of Denmark.

The source preserves a Lithuanian story of the sea goddess Jūratė, whose amber tears follow the death of her mortal beloved Kastytis. Later uses include ornaments, mouthpieces, boxes, figurines, incense, varnish, and pigment-related applications.

## Energetic And Therapeutic Traditions

Therapeutic claims are source-attributed and not medical advice.

### Overview

Amber is treated in gemstone therapy as a warming, protective, calming, and vitality-associated talisman, especially for respiratory and digestive complaints.

### Spiritual

- Associated with the solar-plexus chakra and also the base, sacral, navel, heart, and throat chakras; the source says amber may be placed on all chakras.
- Framed as a strong talisman protecting against negative influences, especially during travel, and against the evil eye.
- Associated with discovering inner wisdom, patience, gentleness, and compassion.
- The source describes programming amber as a magical aid when seeking a partner or new friends.

### Mental

- Helps find the decisiveness and energy to turn plans and wishes into action.
- Associated with balance, harmony, [[concepts/mental/patience|patience]], reduced phobias and irrational [[concepts/emotional/fear|fears]], [[concepts/emotional/trust|trust]], and peacefulness.
- Makes the wearer more aware of ancestors, culture, background, and traditions.
- Linked with increased intellect and [[concepts/mental/creativity|creativity]].

### Emotional

- Amber jewelry is associated with good humor, a sunny feeling, and reduced depressive feelings.
- Linked with calming [[concepts/emotional/fear|fear]] and hysterical tendencies.
- Associated with removing emotional stress.

### Physical

- Described as strongly warming and therefore broadly applicable within the source tradition.
- Linked with [[concepts/physical/lungs|lungs]] and airways, including CARA, asthma, and colds.
- Associated with stomach, intestines, [[concepts/physical/liver|liver]], dark urine, bladder inflammation, and duodenal inflammation; the source mentions amber jewelry and amber water/crystal water.
- Sea-salt-bearing amber is described as being placed on the thyroid for goiter and Graves-Basedow disease claims.
- Linked with hay fever and pollen allergies, teething in young children through amber teething rings, [[concepts/physical/skin|skin]] complaints connected with liver/bile/metabolism, early hearing loss, and external or internal wounds.
- Amber elixir and amber water are source-tradition preparations; these claims are not medical guidance and should not replace professional care.

## Did You Know

- Amber's low density allows it to float in sufficiently salty water.
- Rubbed or heated amber becomes electrically charged; the Greek word *elektron* is linked with this property.
- Baltic amber can be found along the North Sea and Baltic coastlines, especially along the strand line after changing tides.
- Kaliningrad is known as an amber center and has an amber museum.
