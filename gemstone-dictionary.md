# Gemstone Dictionary

This dictionary gathers the recurring language that appears across the gemstone pages: scientific identities, composition terms, visual properties, geological concepts, source roles, and validation checks. It is meant to make the structured parts of the collection easier to read, easier to compare, and easier to grow over time.

Some entries are simple definitions, some are gentle orientation points, and some are clearly waiting to become deeper notes of their own. That is intentional. The dictionary gives the collection a shared vocabulary before every concept needs its own fully developed page.

This dictionary is the interface layer for structured gemstone data.

Gemstone pages should link their structured field labels to the relevant heading in this file. Later, any heading here can stay compact, expand in place, or point onward to a dedicated note without requiring a rewrite of the gemstone pages.

## Core Entries

## Field

The row label used in a two-column structured table.

## Value

The content associated with a structured field.

## Image URL

The canonical source image address captured from the page.

## Alt Text

The alternative text attached to an image, when present.

## Caption

The visible caption or a faithful placeholder when none is listed.

## Dutch Name

The Dutch gemstone name used by the source page.

## English Name

The English gemstone name used for the note title and translation.

## Scientific Name / Mineral Species

The mineralogical species name or the most precise scientific identity available.

Future deep link: [[Scientific Name]]

## Gemstone Variety Of

The parent mineral species or broader mineral identity of which the gemstone is a variety.

## Mineral Group / Family

The mineral family or group to which the stone belongs.

Future deep link: [[Mineral Group]]

## Chemical Class

The chemical classification used in mineralogy, such as tectosilicate, carbonate, or oxide.

Future deep link: [[Chemical Class]]

## Related Stones

Minerals, gemstones, or trade names that are scientifically, visually, or culturally close to the current stone.

## Primary Source

The main source page from which the gemstone note was built.

## Source Notation

The chemical notation preserved exactly as the primary source presents it.

## Normalized Markdown Notation

The same composition expressed cleanly in Markdown, typically with linked elements and readable formula structure.

## Ideal Mineral Formula

The standard mineralogical formula used as the scientific reference point.

Future deep link: [[Mineral Formula]]

## Formula Notes

Short commentary that explains differences between the primary-source notation and external scientific references.

## Trace Elements / Substitutions

Elements or substitutions that modify the ideal formula, color, or structure.

## Inclusions

Minerals, fluids, gases, or structures enclosed within the stone.

## Color-Causing Elements

The elements, inclusions, irradiation effects, or structural factors most responsible for visible color.

## External Validation

A compact statement of which additional sources were used to confirm the composition or classification.

## Property

The row label used in the appearance table.

## Color

The visible color range of the stone.

Future deep link: [[Color]]

## Luster

The way light reflects from the surface, such as vitreous, silky, or pearly.

## Transparency

How much light passes through the material.

## Hardness

The relative scratch resistance of the stone, usually on the Mohs scale.

Future deep link: [[Mohs Hardness]]

## Density

The specific gravity or density range of the mineral.

## Crystal System

The crystallographic system, such as triclinic, monoclinic, or trigonal.

Future deep link: [[Crystal System]]

## Cleavage

The tendency of the mineral to split along structural planes.

## Fracture

How the stone breaks when it does not cleave.

## Habit / Growth Form

The usual crystal habit, aggregate form, or growth expression.

## Distinctive Visual Markers

The quickest visual features that help identify the stone.

## Topic

The row label used in geology and occurrence tables.

## Details

The content paired with a geology or occurrence topic.

## Formation Environment

The geological setting or process through which the stone forms.

## Common Host Rocks

The rocks or deposits in which the mineral commonly occurs.

## Notable Locations

The localities, regions, or countries that matter geologically or historically.

## Mining / Source Notes

Comments about mining, exhaustion, treatment, trade, or source ambiguity.

## Name

The label for a variety, trade name, lookalike, source, or image title, depending on the table.

## Relationship

The type of relation between the named item and the gemstone, such as variety, intergrowth, or lookalike.

## Notes

Short clarification attached to the named item.

## Name Or Term

The specific word whose origin or meaning is being explained.

## Origin / Meaning

The etymology, naming history, or semantic explanation of a name.

## Rune Name

The rune or runes associated with the gemstone.

## Rune Symbol / Image

The source image or symbol reference for the rune.

## Meaning

The traditional meaning associated with the rune or symbolic item.

## Relation To Gemstone

How the rune or symbol is connected to the gemstone in the source tradition.

## Zodiac Sign

The astrological sign associated with the gemstone.

## Mental Concept

A canonical concept describing source-mentioned cognition, attention, learning, perception, motivation, or decision-making.

## Emotional Concept

A canonical concept describing source-mentioned feelings, emotional states, relationships, or emotional processing.

## Physical Concept

A canonical concept describing a source-mentioned body area, system, symptom, function, or condition. Preserve the kind rather than collapsing all physical concepts into generic health.

## Therapeutic Concept ID

The stable dimension-prefixed identifier of a concept, such as `emotional/fear`, `mental/concentration`, or `physical/headache`.

## Therapeutic Relation

The relationship between a gemstone and a concept. Use `associated_with` by default; avoid medical-causation verbs such as `treats`, `cures`, or `prevents`.

## Therapeutic Scope

The provenance boundary of a concept relationship, normally `source-tradition` for claims transferred from Semoea.

## Title

The image title recorded from the source page.

## Section

The section of the gemstone note where an image or statement is primarily used.

## Source

The name of a supporting source.

## URL

The direct link to the supporting source.

## Role

The function of the source in validation, context, or provenance.

## Check

The validation question being answered.

## Result

The answer to the validation check.
